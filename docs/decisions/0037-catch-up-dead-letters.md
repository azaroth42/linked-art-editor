# ADR-0037: Catch-up skips a `done` event it cannot replay as a dead letter

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-11-23 · **Decided by:** the amendment of the Step 1.11 contract for issue #150 (`docs/contracts/1.11-sync-worker.md` §7, Claude Fable 5.2), implemented by Claude Opus 5.5 (Rob Sanderson may override) · **Recorded by:** Claude

## Context
After an index rebuild or restore, `catch_up` replays the `done` events above the lowered watermark (ADR-0034 item 8). It went through `replay`, which raises on the first failure, and only an unavailable QLever was retried. A `done` event that could no longer be replayed (QLever now rejects its update, or its stored revision is missing or unreadable) made the worker exit 1; the event was still `done` and above the watermark at the next start, so the worker crash-looped and no pending event reached QLever again (issue #150, found in the review of PR #148; `docs/questions.md`, Step 1.11 item 7). The issue asked to choose between skipping the event with a dead-letter alarm and refusing with an explicit "rebuild required" state.

## Decision
1. **Skip, not refuse.** In catch-up such an event goes from `done` to `failed`, and catch-up continues. Reasons, data correctness first: after a skip one record is behind in QLever, after a refusal every record published since the lowered watermark is, and every later publish too, until a person rebuilds (hours at scale, no automation before Step 2.18), so the records that differ under "skip" are always a subset of those under "refuse"; the divergence is recorded (`status = 'failed'`, `last_error`, `outbox_failed` > 0, an error log line, `lae sync-worker --failed`); it is repaired by existing means (the record's next event, or any rebuild); and one rule then holds on both paths, so Step 2.17 needs one list of dead letters, not a second state.
2. **`done` → `failed` reuses the dead-letter state and its heal.** The record's next event is `unhealed` and is translated as a `REPLACE` over the versions the dead letter left uncertain (contract items 4 and 5); `lae.sync.events` reads only statuses and is unchanged. Marking any event `failed` is safe for the events after it, because `REPLACE` is always correct (item 6); test 54 checks it over random version chains. After a dead letter catch-up plans again from the watermark, so a later event of the same record is translated after the status change and goes as that `REPLACE`, never as the `DIFF` planned before.
3. **The probe and a second send confirm a rejection, in place of five attempts.** Only two failures are permanent: the event's translation raises at the head of a batch (`LAE-SYNC-001` to `-003`; translation only reads committed rows, so it is not retried), or its update, sent alone, is rejected, then `PROBE_UPDATE` (`DELETE WHERE { <urn:lae:sync-probe> ?p ?o }`, which matches nothing) is acknowledged, then the update is rejected again. A rejected combined update blames no event: its events are sent alone, in order, each recorded on its own. `attempts` is not changed in catch-up: it counts the live deliveries of item 21.
4. **A rejected probe is `unavailable`.** It means the endpoint refuses every update (a wrong update URL answering 404 or 405, a proxy, an incompatible server), so no event may be blamed. `catch_up` raises `UpdatesRefusedError` (a `QLeverError`), which `classify` calls `unavailable` whatever its status; the worker logs `sync_updates_refused` and retries with the usual backoff, and `--once` exits 1. An outage or a refusing endpoint never produces a dead letter in catch-up.
5. **The watermark is not moved by a dead letter.** It keeps meaning the highest id QLever acknowledged to the live index (item 13). A `failed` event is not a `done` event above the watermark, so it never holds catch-up open; a dead letter at the top of the range ends catch-up with the watermark below its id, and the next acknowledged batch moves it past. The dead-letter transaction compares the watermark like every other write (a change rolls back and restarts catch-up) and requires `status = 'done'` on exactly one row.
6. **`replay`, `apply_event` and `--replay-after` are unchanged.** They still raise on the first failure and write nothing; Step 2.18 decides in its own contract what its replay into a second index does with such an event.
7. **Operator surface.** New counters `sync_catch_up_events_total` and `sync_catch_up_dead_letters_total` (a catch-up dead letter also counts in `sync_dead_letters_total`; a probe is not counted in `updates_sent_total`; a retried catch-up counts in `sync_unavailable_total`); the `/metrics` listener starts before the first catch-up; `last_error` of a catch-up dead letter is the `LAE-SYNC-00x` string with `"catch_up": true`; `sync_dead_letter` log lines carry `during` (`catch_up` or `live`); `lae sync-worker --failed` lists the dead letters as JSON lines. The operator action is in `deploy/README.md`, "Dead letters in the sync queue".

## Alternatives considered
- **Refuse with a "rebuild required" state.** Correct but stops sync for every record until a person acts, and needs a state the admin UI does not have yet (Step 2.17).
- **Count catch-up failures toward the five attempts.** Five restarts, each with a full replay, before the queue moves; and the attempts would mix two meanings.
- **Dead-letter on the first rejection.** A transient refusal of the whole endpoint (a misconfigured URL, a proxy) would blame events one by one. The probe tells the endpoint from the event; the second send rules out a refusal that has already passed.
- **Move the watermark past the dead letter.** It would no longer mean "acknowledged", and Step 2.18 relies on that meaning.
- **A probe on the live path too.** Left out: item 21 is unchanged. Recorded for the owner (`docs/questions.md`, Step 1.11 item 9).

## Consequences
- The worker no longer crash-loops on a `done` event it cannot replay; one record stays behind until its next publish or a rebuild, and `outbox_failed` raises the alarm.
- A damaged revision keeps its record behind, and makes the record's later events dead letters too (in catch-up and live), until the `record_revisions` row is restored (contract item 33; recorded for Step 2.18, `docs/questions.md`, Step 1.11 item 8).
- No migration, no new `LAE-SYNC` code, and no change to `lae.sync.events`, `lae.sync.qlever_client`, `process_batch` or contracts 1.10 and 1.14.

Evidence: `backend/tests/sync/test_batch.py` (tests 38–53: the poisoned world, the probe and the second send, transient failures at four points, a refused probe, the watermark compared during judgement, the running process and its metrics, `--once`, `replay`, `--failed`, the token), `backend/tests/sync/test_replay_property.py` (test 54), `backend/tests/sync/test_qlever_integration.py` (test 55, QLever 0.6.0's own 400 and the probe, run locally).
